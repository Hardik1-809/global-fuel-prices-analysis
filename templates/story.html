Core Investigation Themes & Key Findings

Synthesizing visual observations from the Tableau Story. (Analytical findings dynamically referenced from Tableau visual trends):

Point 01
Crude Price Pass-Through

Evaluating how changes in the Brent crude oil benchmark (USD/barrel) reflect on retail petrol and diesel pump prices across different national markets.

Empirical Observation

[Observed in Tableau Story: Pass-through elasticity is asymmetric; crude price hikes translate rapidly to pump rates, while downward corrections reflect regional lag].

Point 02
Taxation vs. Subsidy Regimes

Investigating why consumer pump prices vary substantially between nations facing identical global crude commodity procurement costs.

Empirical Observation

[Observed in Tableau Story: Fiscal policy (excise taxes, VAT, vs. subsidy caps) accounts for the largest portion of global pump price dispersion].

Point 03
Income Tier Vulnerability

Cross-referencing price indices with World Bank income tiers (High, Upper-Middle, Lower-Middle) to identify consumer purchasing pressure.

Empirical Observation

[Observed in Tableau Story: Lower-middle income economies exhibit heightened sensitivity to global price volatility unless buffered by fiscal subsidies].

Project Summary & Takeaway

Key Synthesis & Policy Implications

The visual evidence synthesized across this Tableau Story demonstrates that global fuel prices are not dictated solely by geological scarcity or Brent crude benchmarks. Instead, retail energy costs represent a composite equation where sovereign taxation regimes, green energy transition policies, and socioeconomic subsidy structures play a determinative role.
